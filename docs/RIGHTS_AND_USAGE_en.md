## Attribution and scholarly citation

When redistributing software, retain the copyright notice and full MIT license. MIT permits modification and commercial reuse without separately requiring a paper citation. Scholarly citation is requested here as a research practice, rather than added as a restriction on MIT permissions. [MIT terms](https://opensource.org/license/mit)

When reusing original derived data or documentation, provide appropriate author/title credit, link to CC BY 4.0 and indicate modifications. Commercial reuse is permitted. A suitable attribution pattern is:

> Source: Shengyu Zou and coauthors; SWOT satellite discharge estimates with strict and retained quality-control variants accompanying manuscript v15, Version 1.1.0. License: CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Public source: insert the actual repository URL or version DOI. Changes: describe your modifications, or state that the material was unchanged.

The citation files provide the complete ten-author list; bibliographies may format it according to journal style. Attribution must not imply author endorsement. Credit upstream resources separately according to their own recommendations. [CC BY 4.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Preserve the version record

Keep source files, Git history, approved tags, distribution archives and SHA256 hashes. These support verification of the content present in a particular version. A Zenodo DOI identifies an archived research object and improves citability; it does not itself establish copyright ownership.

These notices cover original expression and applicable rights. They do not assert exclusive ownership of public satellite inputs, factual numerical values or algorithmic ideas. The release grants no permission to publish the restricted ground data that it excludes.

CC BY permissions already granted to compliant users generally cannot be withdrawn simply because the author later changes their preference. If commercial restrictions are intended, the rights holders should determine an alternative license before the first public release, rather than adding conflicting terms to MIT or CC BY. This local draft keeps the existing MIT/CC BY 4.0 approach. [Official CC terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
